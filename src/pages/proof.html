Proof

The standard, and how to check it.

We hold this site and our own mail to the checklist we bring to yours. Everything below is public. Nothing here asks you to trust us.

01 · This site

  • Every connection to this site is encrypted, and browsers are told to insist on it for a year, subdomains included (HSTS).
  • The page may run no code except its own and loads nothing from anyone else — not a font, not a script, not an image. A Content Security Policy enforces it, and forbids any other site from framing this one.
  • No cookies, no trackers, no third-party requests. Nothing you do here is reported to anyone.
  • If you choose light or dark, that choice is kept in your own browser and is never sent anywhere.
  • The site is static files on a content-delivery network: there is no server of ours to break into and no database behind the pages.
  • The fonts are served from here, under the SIL Open Font License.

02 · Our mail

  • Mail from theclauditor.ai is signed (DKIM) and policed (SPF and DMARC), so a receiver can tell forged mail from ours.
  • Other mail servers are given a published policy for delivering to us over encrypted connections (MTA-STS, in its testing phase) and asked to report any failure (TLS-RPT).

03 · Our domain

  • Only the certificate authorities we name may issue certificates for our domain (CAA).
  • Our DNS answers are signed, so they cannot be quietly swapped on the way to you (DNSSEC).

04 · If you find something

A security.txt file tells researchers where to report a problem, with a PGP key for the conversation and a written policy behind it — including a safe-harbor promise.

05 · The source is public

The source of this site — every page, the stylesheet, the fonts, the build and check scripts, and the scan that re-reads the live headers — is a public repository: github.com/The-Clauditor/theclauditor-ai. Commits are signed. On every change, and on a schedule, a public workflow rebuilds the site, checks it against the rules on this page, and scans the live site's headers, mail and DNS records; the log of each run is public.

A change in the repository does not deploy anything. The site is published by hand, by its owner, from a build whose checks have passed.

The assessment methodology itself is not in that repository. It stays private, available to a reviewer on request.

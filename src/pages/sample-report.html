The short version

We looked at the firm's public footprint (alderfinch.example and its mail), the website, the two Macs and two phones the partners work from, and the accounts behind them. Overall, Alder & Finch is in fair shape: a reputable mail provider and encrypted laptops are already in place, and nothing we found puts money or client files at immediate risk. The single most important thing to do first is turn on hardware security keys for the two Google accounts and the recovery address behind them — the firm's client files, mail and bank sign-ins all hang off those accounts.

Below is what's already working, the handful of things worth fixing, and where we'd start.

What's strong

  • Mail runs on Google Workspace with two-step verification on both partner accounts — the single biggest protection a small firm can have is already on.
  • Both laptops are encrypted (FileVault) and on the current macOS — a lost laptop is an inconvenience, not a breach.
  • The website is served over HTTPS only, with a current certificate and TLS 1.3, and no exposed backup, configuration or source-control files were found.

Scope & method (sample)

What we assessed. The website at alderfinch.example: server and stack fingerprint, TLS posture, HTTP security headers, cookies, exposed sensitive files, content-system posture, the contact form and the personal data it collects, and third-party trackers — against OWASP Top 10:2025, ASVS 5.0 and WSTG.

How we assessed it. A passive-first pass over what the site already serves to the public, followed — only inside the signed authorization — by authorization-gated path checks and an active TLS scan, tagged as such in the evidence. We read; we did not exploit.

What we did not assess. The bookkeeping platform itself (a vendor's system), the bank portals, and any host not named in the authorization.

Authorization. AUTH-SAMPLE-001, signed by M. Alder, naming alderfinch.example and the four devices. No system outside that scope was probed.

Evidence & retention. Redacted header blocks and status lines — never a secret, a token or real personal data — held in the engagement's encrypted store for 90 days after delivery, then destroyed.

What a real report has that this sample doesn't: the evidence exhibits (redacted screenshots and command output), the full findings list, and the client's own strengths — which are always specific, never "good job".
